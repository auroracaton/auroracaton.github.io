Machine Learning Research: Drug-Induced Autoimmunity Prediction

Developing and evaluating a deep neural network to identify compounds associated with Drug-Induced Autoimmunity using molecular descriptors, threshold optimization, and cross-validation.

Python TensorFlow Scikit-learn Deep Learning Healthcare Analytics
01 · THE CHALLENGE

Predicting Drug Safety Risk Earlier

Drug-Induced Autoimmunity occurs when a medication triggers the immune system to attack healthy tissue. Identifying this risk earlier in the drug-development process may help researchers recognize potentially problematic compounds before they reach later stages of testing.

For this research project, I developed a deep neural network to classify chemical compounds as DIA-positive or DIA-negative using numerical molecular descriptors. A major challenge was the imbalance between positive and negative cases, which could cause the model to achieve high accuracy while still failing to detect the compounds of greatest concern.

03 · RESULTS

Results and Key Findings

The final analysis showed that strong overall accuracy did not necessarily mean that the model was effectively identifying DIA-positive compounds. Threshold selection and sensitivity were therefore critical parts of the evaluation.

BEST DATA SPLIT

75 / 25

The 75/25 train-test split produced the strongest overall performance across the evaluation metrics.

FINAL ARCHITECTURE

2 Hidden Layers

The best-performing model used two hidden layers, 16 units per layer, and a dropout rate of 0.3.

OPTIMIZED THRESHOLD

0.3

Reducing the threshold from 0.5 to 0.3 improved the model’s ability to detect DIA-positive compounds.

CROSS-VALIDATION

≈ 80%

Five-fold stratified cross-validation produced an average accuracy of approximately 80%.

KEY TAKEAWAY

Accuracy alone did not tell the full story.

At the default threshold, the model showed a strong bias toward negative predictions and nearly failed to detect positive cases. Lowering the threshold improved sensitivity, demonstrating the importance of selecting metrics and decision rules that reflect the real scientific objective.

05 · REFLECTION

What I Learned

This project strengthened my understanding of deep learning, model evaluation, and the importance of choosing metrics that reflect the purpose of a model. Although the initial model achieved reasonable accuracy, its low sensitivity revealed that it was not effectively identifying DIA-positive compounds.

Experimenting with classification thresholds demonstrated how modeling decisions can create important trade-offs between sensitivity, specificity, and overall accuracy. The project also strengthened my ability to validate results, interpret model limitations, and communicate technical findings through a formal research poster.
